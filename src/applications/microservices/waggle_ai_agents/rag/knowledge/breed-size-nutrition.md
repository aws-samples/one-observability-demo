# Nutrition by Breed Size

**Large and giant breeds (Labrador, German Shepherd, Great Dane, Mastiff)** are prone
to hip and elbow dysplasia and joint problems. Large-breed puppy formulas control
calcium and calorie density to slow growth to a healthy rate — rapid growth worsens
skeletal disease. Adults benefit from glucosamine, chondroitin, and omega-3s for joints,
and from controlled weight to reduce load on hips.

**Small and toy breeds (Chihuahua, Pomeranian, Yorkshire Terrier)** have fast
metabolisms and small stomachs, so they need calorie-dense, small-kibble food fed in
several small meals. Toy-breed puppies are prone to hypoglycemia and need frequent
feeding. Small breeds are also prone to dental disease, so kibble shape and dental
support matter.

**Medium breeds** generally do well on standard adult maintenance formulas matched to
activity level.

Kibble size and shape should match the mouth: too-large kibble discourages small dogs,
while very small kibble may be gulped whole by large dogs.
