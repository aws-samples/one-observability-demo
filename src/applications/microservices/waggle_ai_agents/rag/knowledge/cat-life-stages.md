# Cat Nutrition by Life Stage

Cats are **obligate carnivores** — they require animal-based protein and the amino acid
taurine, which must come from the diet. Taurine deficiency causes heart disease
(dilated cardiomyopathy) and blindness.

**Kittens (0–12 months)** need high-protein, calorie-dense growth food and more frequent
meals. Protein 35–50% dry matter.

**Adult cats (1–7 years)** need high-protein maintenance diets. Because cats evolved to
get water from prey, many benefit from wet food to support hydration and urinary health.

**Senior cats (7+ years)** often lose muscle mass; they need highly digestible protein
and monitoring for kidney disease, hyperthyroidism, and dental problems. Do not
restrict protein unless a vet diagnoses advanced kidney disease.

Cats should never be fed dog food long-term — it lacks sufficient taurine and the
protein levels cats require.
